The artist

Drawing has always been where I slow down.

I work almost entirely in a spiral sketchbook — ballpoint, fineliner and the occasional wash of ink or watercolor. No digital undo button, no layers. Just paper, patience and enough cross-hatching to build a face out of thousands of tiny lines.

My subjects move between two worlds: the mythology and freedom fighters I grew up hearing about, and the anime and manga characters I grew up watching. Both get the same care — the same slow build of tone, the same attention to the eyes, because that's always where a portrait lives or dies.

How a page gets made

Five stages, one sketchbook

Every drawing follows roughly the same path from blank page to finished portrait.

01

Reference & thumbnail

A loose, light pencil map of proportions before any ink touches the page.

02

Line & structure

Confident fineliner or ballpoint lines lock in the features — no going back after this.

03

Cross-hatching

Layers of hatching build up shadow and form, one direction of line at a time.

04

Wash & color

Ink wash, watercolor or marker adds selective warmth — gold, vermillion, sepia.

05

Sign & scan

A signature, a date, and the page joins the rest of the sketchbook.
